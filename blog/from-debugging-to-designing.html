From Debugging Code to Designing Experiences

I didn’t set out to care about pixels. I set out to solve problems. Somewhere along the way, the two became the same thing.

Jan 2025 4 min read Career Design

Where it started

Like most people in tech, I didn’t have a perfectly planned path. I had curiosity and a laptop. My early days were less about building beautiful things and more about making things work — debugging until 2am, reading documentation I barely understood, copy-pasting Stack Overflow answers and hoping for the best.

The first time something I built actually ran without errors, I felt like I’d discovered fire. That feeling never really goes away. It just gets more refined.

The turn I didn’t expect

Somewhere between writing research papers and shipping side projects, I started noticing something. The technical part — the logic, the data, the architecture — that was learnable. What was harder, and more interesting, was the experience of using something.

Why did one interface feel effortless and another feel like a chore? Why did some projects feel alive and others feel dead on arrival? I started caring about the gap between what something does and how it feels. That shift changed how I approach everything.

The best code is the code no one notices — because the experience is so smooth, the technology disappears.

What building this portfolio taught me

Recently I’ve been rebuilding my personal portfolio from scratch — projects, publications, gallery, blog, the whole thing. What started as a simple “I need a website” exercise turned into months of thinking about design systems, hover states, information architecture, and what it actually means to present your work well.

Every detail I sweated over — the way a card lifts on hover, the way a timeline dot pulses, the way an image lightbox opens — taught me something about intentionality. Nothing should be there by accident. It also taught me that shipping imperfect things is infinitely more valuable than perfecting things that never ship.

Where I am now

I’m at a place where I enjoy both sides — the rigorous, analytical side that writes research and solves hard technical problems, and the craft side that obsesses over whether a font pairing feels right.

I don’t think these are opposites. The best engineers I admire think like designers. The best designers I admire think like engineers.

What I’d tell my earlier self

Stop waiting until it’s perfect. Put it out there. The feedback you get from something real will teach you more than another month of private iteration ever will.

And care about the details — not because anyone will always notice them, but because the habit of caring about small things is what produces big things worth noticing.
